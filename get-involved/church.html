Bring It to Your Church

Help a congregation support children and families with humility, safeguards, and reliable local partnerships.

Overview

Churches can offer meals, transportation, support groups, practical help, mentoring through screened programs, meeting space, and steady friendship. The strongest ministries follow child-safety policies, work with qualified agencies, respect people of every background, and support birth, kinship, foster, and adoptive families without turning private stories into publicity.

Realistic expectations

  • A child-protection policy, screening, training, and reporting procedure come before recruitment.
  • Not every family wants public prayer, testimony, photographs, or disclosure. Ask permission and protect privacy.
  • Support should continue when placements change or reunification occurs.
  • Leave One Light On is not a church-placement program or ministry-certification body.

Frequently asked questions

Should we recruit foster parents from the pulpit?

Awareness can help, but informed orientation and honest expectations must follow. Do not pressure people into a life-changing commitment.

Can volunteers work directly with children?

Only through programs with appropriate screening, training, supervision, consent, and insurance.

What can a small church do?

Choose one local partner and meet one recurring need well: meals, a support-group room, school supplies requested by families, or transportation coordinated by the agency.

Your practical first step

Begin here.

Ask the church’s safeguarding leader and one licensed local agency to identify a single, recurring need the congregation can meet for six months.
