Become a Mentor

Find a screened, supported mentoring program near you and understand the commitment before saying yes.

Overview

Mentoring is a consistent, relationship-centered commitment. Good programs screen, train, match, supervise, and support mentors. The goal is not to rescue or replace a family; it is to become a dependable adult who listens, encourages, keeps boundaries, and follows program safeguards.

Realistic expectations

  • Programs commonly require an application, interview, references, background screening, and training.
  • Consistency matters more than grand gestures. Expect a commitment measured in months, not one event.
  • You must follow confidentiality, transportation, communication, social-media, and reporting rules.
  • A mentor is not a therapist, caseworker, parent, or emergency service.

Frequently asked questions

How much time does mentoring take?

Commitments vary by program. Ask about frequency, duration, supervision, and what happens when life changes.

Can I mentor independently?

Working through a reputable program protects young people and volunteers through screening, training, boundaries, and support.

What if I have no special expertise?

Reliability, listening, humility, and willingness to learn are often more important than a particular profession.

Your practical first step

Begin here.

Search the MENTOR Connector by ZIP code. Contact two programs and compare screening, training, time commitment, and ongoing support.
