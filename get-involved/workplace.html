Bring It to Your Workplace

Turn employment, mentoring, flexibility, and practical support into a responsible workplace commitment.

Overview

Employers can help without turning the workplace into a political campaign. Useful approaches include recruiting young adults with foster-care experience, paid internships, mentorship through vetted partners, supervisor training, predictable scheduling, education support, donated professional services, and flexibility for employees serving as caregivers or CASA volunteers.

Realistic expectations

  • Start with one measurable program and a qualified community partner.
  • Protect privacy; employees and young people should never be pressured to disclose foster-care history.
  • Youth employment must follow federal and state labor laws.
  • Avoid inspirational storytelling without compensation, consent, and real opportunity.

Frequently asked questions

Must we create a new nonprofit program?

No. Most employers should partner with experienced organizations and strengthen existing hiring, mentoring, benefit, and volunteer systems.

What is the easiest responsible first step?

Name an internal owner, identify one qualified local partner, and define a small 90-day pilot with measurable participation and retention.

Can employees volunteer during work hours?

That depends on company policy and the partner’s requirements. Paid volunteer time can help, but consistency and screening still matter.

Your practical first step

Begin here.

Invite HR, community engagement, and one operational leader to a 30-minute meeting. Choose one pathway: hiring, mentoring, caregiver support, or skills-based volunteering.
