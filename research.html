Evidence guide

Foster Care 101

Foster care is temporary out-of-home care used when a child cannot safely remain with a parent or caregiver. Depending on the jurisdiction and the child’s needs, placement may be with relatives, family friends, licensed foster families, or residential settings.

Read the guide →

Evidence guide

Why Children Enter Foster Care

Children usually come to the attention of child welfare after concerns about abuse or neglect. A report may be screened out, investigated, or handled through an alternative response. Out-of-home care is considered when safety cannot be maintained at home.

Read the guide →

Evidence guide

Foster Home Availability

Communities often describe a “foster home shortage,” but the practical issue is not only the number of beds. Agencies need appropriate families for a child’s age, sibling group, location, culture, medical needs, school stability, and permanency plan.

Evidence guide

Aging Out of Foster Care

Depending on state law and eligibility, some young people leave foster care between ages 18 and 21 without reunification, adoption, guardianship, or another legally permanent family.

Evidence guide

Education Outcomes

Students in foster care may experience school changes, record delays, transportation barriers, lost credits, trauma, and changes in adult support. These disruptions can affect attendance, progress, graduation, and postsecondary planning.
