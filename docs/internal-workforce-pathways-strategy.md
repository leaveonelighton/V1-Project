# Internal Strategy Note: Careers, Skills & Opportunity

This note is internal governance material. It guides future review of the Careers, Skills & Opportunity category and is not public website copy.

## NOTICE

Begin with a recognizable human need or possibility. People may be looking for useful work, a new skill, greater stability, a different direction, or one practical place to begin. Human dignity comes before productivity, and useful work takes many forms.

## ENTER

Offer a calm, welcoming entry point without pressure. Visitors should be free to explore several respectable options according to their circumstances, interests, responsibilities, and goals. Leave One Light On points toward independent resources; it does not act as an employer, school, placement service, scholarship administrator, credentialing body, or workforce authority.

## DISCOVER

Help visitors understand what each independent resource offers, whom it may serve, and what limits or eligibility conditions may apply. Present careers, skilled trades, apprenticeships, technical education, scholarships, training, job-search tools, and career exploration as possible directions rather than promised solutions.

No employment, eligibility, admission, scholarship, certification, income, or training result is guaranteed. The movement and its founder are not the heroes, and the listed organizations are not presented as saviors. The visitor retains agency.

## PARTICIPATE

Favor one credible, practical next step over grand promises. A visitor might read current requirements, explore a career field, find a local program, compare training options, or decide that none of the listed choices fits. Each is a valid exercise of choice.

## CARRY THE LIGHT

Someone who receives useful guidance may later choose to help someone else. That possibility should remain an invitation, never an obligation or a claim that the movement produced the outcome.

## Modern-Parable Review Standard

Future editors should ask:

1. Does this begin with a recognizable human need or possibility?
2. Does it offer more than one respectable path?
3. Does it preserve the visitor's freedom to choose?
4. Does it provide at least one concrete next step?
5. Does it avoid making Leave One Light On, Ted Johnson, or a listed organization the hero?
6. Does it avoid guarantees and inflated promises?
7. Does it leave room for the visitor to help someone else later?
8. Is the public language practical rather than poetic, preachy, or vague?

## Guardrails

1. Keep the language warm, grounded, practical, hopeful, and nonpartisan.
2. Public wording may be faith-informed but must not make theological claims.
3. Do not quote, imitate, or present the material as a biblical parable.
4. Do not overuse imagery involving lamps, doors, rooms, roads, or paths.
5. Describe independent resources accurately and neutrally without implying partnership, sponsorship, endorsement, or affiliation.
6. State important limits plainly. Do not imply guaranteed employment, eligibility, admission, scholarships, certification, income, training, services, or results.
7. Add or expand public material only through a separate owner-reviewed patch.
