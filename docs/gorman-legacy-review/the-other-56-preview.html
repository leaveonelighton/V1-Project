THE FIFTY-SEVEN

Fifty-seven individual lives.

The number gets attention, but it is not the whole story.

Each person who passed through Cedar Hill brought a different history, personality, need, hope, and experience. No single account can speak for everyone.

Their stories belong to them.

Every person connected to Cedar Hill owns their own story. This site will not tell those stories without permission.

Ted can share what he remembers and what the experience meant in his life. Others may choose to speak in their own time, in their own words—or not at all.

Both choices deserve respect.

THE OTHER 56

“I was the first. But I wasn’t the last.”
— Ted Johnson, reflecting on his own experience

Margaret “Peg” Gorman and Jeremy “Jerry” Gorman fostered fifty-seven children over the years. Ted identifies himself, from firsthand experience, as their first official foster child. He can share what happened to him. The other fifty-six have their own histories and experiences.

Those stories belong to them. They may choose to share anonymously, use a name or pseudonym, or keep their experiences private. Every choice deserves respect. No one is expected to have had the same experience or outcome.

The total of fifty-seven is documented in Margaret Gorman’s obituary, published by the Brattleboro Reformer on November 28–29, 2003. Ted’s place as the first official foster child is his own firsthand account, shared here with his permission.

It started with room for one more. It didn’t end there.

Making room for first-person stories

If members of the fifty-seven or people directly connected to Cedar Hill choose to contribute, their accounts should be presented with informed consent, clear attribution, and control over what is shared.

The purpose is not to turn private lives into content. It is to preserve history without taking ownership of someone else’s experience.

Story sharing is being prepared. No new personal account will be published without consent and owner moderation. There is no obligation to contribute.
