Choose one action

Small acts of compassion are still acts of compassion.

Send the first message

Text someone: “Thinking of you today. No need to reply.”

Make room

Invite someone to share a meal, a cup of coffee, or a seat at the table.

Listen without fixing

Give someone your full attention and let their story matter.

Offer practical help

Run an errand, give a ride, deliver a meal, or help with one immediate need.

Support a foster family

Ask a local foster-care organization what help is genuinely needed this week.

Connect someone with help

When the need is beyond you, help the person reach trusted professional or crisis support.

Keep it simple.

Notice. Care. Take one step. You are not being asked to become someone’s rescuer—only to practice compassion faithfully.

A quiet act of compassion

Give Blood. Share Life.

Blood donation is a quiet act of compassion for someone you may never meet. Hospitals depend on volunteer donors every day to care for accident victims, surgical patients, mothers, premature babies, cancer patients, and people living with serious blood disorders.

Blood donation facts

  • Someone in the United States needs blood or platelets approximately every two seconds.
  • Only about 3% of age-eligible Americans donate blood in a typical year.
  • Blood and platelets cannot be manufactured; volunteer donors are essential.
  • Every blood type is needed.
  • O-positive is one of the most frequently used blood types in hospital care.
