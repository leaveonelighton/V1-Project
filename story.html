A Story in the Light

The Door They Opened

One ordinary act of compassion changed my life.

When I was thirteen years old, I arrived at 5 Cedar Hill Road frightened and searching for somewhere to belong.

Peg and Jerry Gorman did not make grand promises. They simply opened their door, made room at their table, and treated me like I mattered.

They could not erase everything that had happened to me. But they noticed me—and they acted.

That is compassion.

Compassion does not require us to solve every problem. That is God's work. Compassion asks us to notice the person in front of us and do the next kind thing we are able to do.

God used one family's open door to change my life. Their simple act of compassion continues to inspire me today.

Who might need you to notice them?

Meet Ted

Leaving a Light On Through Blood Donation

Ted is a longtime blood donor and an advocate for giving whenever health and eligibility permit. As an O-positive donor, he understands that his blood type is frequently needed in trauma care and can help patients with positive blood types.

For Ted, donating blood is one of the purest forms of service. You may never know the recipient’s name, hear their story, or receive their thanks. You simply give because somewhere, someone’s life depends upon a stranger choosing to help.

Scripture reminds us that “the life of the flesh is in the blood.” — Leviticus 17:11. For Ted, that truth is also a call to action: when we are able, we can quietly share the gift of life with another human being.

Blood cannot be manufactured. It must be given. Leaving one light on can begin with rolling up one sleeve.
