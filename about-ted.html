About Ted

A life shaped by learning differently, belonging, teaching, technology, second chances, and the people who chose to believe in him.

Someone left a light on for him.

Theodore “Ted” Johnson arrived at Cedar Hill Road at thirteen. Peg and Jerry Gorman’s welcome became an inheritance he now carries through his books and the Leave One Light On movement.

Learning became part of the story, too.

Ted’s path was not defined by one institution or one conventional route. He learned through experience, persistence, technology, work, teaching, and the confidence of people who helped him see possibilities that were not always obvious at first.

As an author and former IT instructor, Ted came to value a simple principle: people do not all learn the same way, grow on the same timetable, or need the same kind of support. Good education starts by seeing the person before the label, the obstacle, or the outcome.

Why the mission is broader than one cause.

Foster care is part of Ted’s story, but it is not the whole of it. Leave One Light On also grows from experiences with education, learning barriers, technology, work, mentorship, health, faith, loss, second chances, and the importance of being believed in.

Those experiences do not make Ted the authority on every subject. They provide the questions. The organization’s responsibility is to test those questions against trustworthy research, established practice, and the knowledge of qualified people and organizations before turning them into public guidance.

The person behind the movement

Ted is the founder and steward of Leave One Light On. He publishes in his own name and accepts responsibility for the movement’s stories, research standards, corrections, and public commitments.

This is a growing educational nonprofit built from lived experience, but it is not a biography project. Its credibility must come from transparent sourcing, honest limits, responsible partnerships, and actions that can be verified—not from promises larger than the work.

Questions, corrections, and partnership inquiries are welcome at contact@leaveonelighton.org.

The movement is not a promise that Ted or this website can solve every need. It is an invitation to learn, connect with qualified organizations, and choose one responsible act of compassion.
