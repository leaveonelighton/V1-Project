Foster Home Availability

Research & learning

A plain-language evidence guide from Leave One Light On.

Reviewed July 2026 · U.S. scope

What is the issue?

Communities often describe a “foster home shortage,” but the practical issue is not only the number of beds. Agencies need appropriate families for a child’s age, sibling group, location, culture, medical needs, school stability, and permanency plan.

Why does it matter?

When the right family setting is unavailable, children may be placed far from school and relationships, separated from siblings, moved repeatedly, or cared for in a more restrictive setting than needed.

What does current evidence say?

There is no single national shortage figure that describes every state and every placement need. AFCARS can show national and state placement patterns, while state agencies publish local recruitment priorities. Responsible reporting should distinguish general capacity from the availability of well-supported homes able to meet specific needs.

What is one meaningful step I can take today?

Turn learning into one practical action.

Ask your state or local agency which placement needs are hardest to meet—and what support existing foster and kinship families need to remain available.

Read this responsibly

  • Do not apply population-level findings to an individual.
  • State and tribal systems differ.
  • Data definitions and reporting years vary.
  • People with lived experience are more than an outcome measure.
