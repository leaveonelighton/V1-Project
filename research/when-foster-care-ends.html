When Foster Care Ends, Opportunity Shouldn’t

Hope and Practical Compassion

November 10, 2026 · Hope and Practical Compassion

On November 7, 2026, First Lady Melania Trump and Louisiana State University announced an encouraging investment: a $7.2 million endowed scholarship fund for students who have experienced foster care. LSU says the endowment is expected to provide around $288,000 each year and could help with living costs as well as educational expenses.

That is welcome news. But it also raises a question that reaches beyond one campus:

What happens to young people when foster care ends?

A young adult may need tuition assistance, but also a place to live, transportation, a job lead, someone to call, and encouragement when life gets difficult. A scholarship can open a door. Someone still has to help a young person walk through it.

The U.S. Government Accountability Office documented unmet needs in housing and education assistance for young people transitioning out of foster care. That research is a reminder that generosity, smart administration, and practical follow-through must work together.

A thousand dollars—and something beyond money

Our founder, Ted Johnson, was the first official foster child welcomed into the Gorman family home. He has reflected on what even $1,000 might have meant to him as a child.

But there is an important distinction: the federal $1,000 Trump Account contribution is not automatically available to all foster children. It applies to children meeting the IRS's age, citizenship, Social Security number and election requirements. Programs should be understood as they actually work, not simply as the headlines describe them.

What can one person do?

Perhaps you cannot endow a scholarship. Could you help a young adult prepare a résumé? Share a verified resource? Support a foster family? Mentor through a trusted organization? Make an introduction that opens an opportunity?

A home can change a life. So can an education. And sometimes, so can one person who makes room.

Commit to One. Notice the need. Take one meaningful step.
