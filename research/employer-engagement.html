Employer Engagement

A plain-language evidence guide from Leave One Light On.

Reviewed July 2026 · U.S. scope

What is the issue?

Employers often want to help but may default to one-time drives or inspirational events rather than durable opportunity.

Why does it matter?

Employment, paid learning, mentors, supervisor quality, and predictable systems can create practical stability. The workplace can help without asking employees or applicants to disclose private foster-care history.

What does current evidence say?

Federal resources emphasize lawful youth employment, career and technical education, apprenticeships, and transition services. Evidence does not support a single universal employer model; responsible programs define a target population, qualified partner, safeguards, paid opportunity, support, and measurable outcomes.

What is one meaningful step I can take today?

Turn learning into one practical action.

Choose one 90-day pilot: a paid internship, skills-based volunteer partnership, caregiver-support policy review, or mentor recruitment through a screened program.

Read this responsibly

  • Do not apply population-level findings to an individual.
  • State and tribal systems differ.
  • Data definitions and reporting years vary.
  • People with lived experience are more than an outcome measure.
