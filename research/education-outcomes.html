Education Outcomes

A plain-language evidence guide from Leave One Light On.

Reviewed July 2026 · U.S. scope

What is the issue?

Students in foster care may experience school changes, record delays, transportation barriers, lost credits, trauma, and changes in adult support. These disruptions can affect attendance, progress, graduation, and postsecondary planning.

Why does it matter?

School can be a source of stability. Federal education law includes protections intended to support school stability and immediate enrollment for students in foster care.

What does current evidence say?

The U.S. Department of Education maintains state foster-care points of contact and guidance on education stability, agency collaboration, data sharing, career and technical education, and transition planning. NYTD also collects education-related outcomes from current and former foster youth.

What is one meaningful step I can take today?

Turn learning into one practical action.

Find your state education foster-care point of contact and ask the school district who serves as the local foster-care liaison.

Read this responsibly

  • Do not apply population-level findings to an individual.
  • State and tribal systems differ.
  • Data definitions and reporting years vary.
  • People with lived experience are more than an outcome measure.
