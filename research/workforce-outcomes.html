Workforce Outcomes

Research & learning

A plain-language evidence guide from Leave One Light On.

Reviewed July 2026 · U.S. scope

What is the issue?

Young adults with foster-care experience may enter work while also managing housing, transportation, education, health care, documentation, and limited family support.

Why does it matter?

A first job can provide income, structure, references, skills, and belonging. A poor first experience can add instability. Employers influence retention through ordinary practices such as predictable scheduling, clear supervision, fair pay, transportation awareness, and access to training.

What does current evidence say?

NYTD measures employment and financial outcomes over time. Federal transition programs support education and work preparation, while the Department of Labor provides youth-employment requirements and apprenticeship resources. Outcomes vary; foster-care history should never be treated as a prediction about an individual.

What is one meaningful step I can take today?

Turn learning into one practical action.

Help one young adult create a resume, secure identification, practice an interview, or connect with a legitimate apprenticeship or workforce program.

Read this responsibly

  • Do not apply population-level findings to an individual.
  • State and tribal systems differ.
  • Data definitions and reporting years vary.
  • People with lived experience are more than an outcome measure.
