Foster Care 101

A plain-language evidence guide from Leave One Light On.

Reviewed July 2026 · U.S. scope

What is the issue?

Foster care is temporary out-of-home care used when a child cannot safely remain with a parent or caregiver. Depending on the jurisdiction and the child’s needs, placement may be with relatives, family friends, licensed foster families, or residential settings.

Why does it matter?

Separation is consequential even when necessary for safety. Children need stability, relationships, education continuity, health care, cultural connection, and a path toward safe permanency.

What does current evidence say?

Federal guidance describes child welfare as a continuum that includes prevention, child protection, in-home support, temporary out-of-home care, reunification, and other permanency options. When children enter care, safe reunification is generally the first priority; kinship care is preferred when appropriate because it can preserve family and cultural connections. AFCARS is the federal case-level data system used to describe children in foster care and adoption.

What is one meaningful step I can take today?

Turn learning into one practical action.

Learn the difference between temporary foster care and adoption. Then use your state’s official information session rather than relying on social-media anecdotes.

Read this responsibly

  • Do not apply population-level findings to an individual.
  • State and tribal systems differ.
  • Data definitions and reporting years vary.
  • People with lived experience are more than an outcome measure.
