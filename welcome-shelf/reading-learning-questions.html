Reading & Learning: Questions to Ask

A one-page companion to the living Dyslexia & Reading Difficulties evidence guide.

Start with a useful question.
What part of reading is difficult, and what evaluation or instruction would help clarify the need?

1. What am I noticing?

Check what seems relevant. A difficulty alone does not establish a diagnosis.

  • Connecting letters and sounds or decoding words
  • Reading words accurately
  • Reading fluently or quickly enough
  • Spelling
  • Understanding what was read
  • Something else I want clarified

2. What should I not assume?

  • Reading difficulty is not a measure of intelligence, motivation, character, or potential.
  • Not every reading difficulty is dyslexia.
  • Dyslexia does not look identical in every person.
  • Grades alone do not explain the underlying reading need.

3. Questions to bring to the conversation

4. Bring what helps clarify the picture

  • Examples of reading or spelling work
  • Notes about what seems easier or harder
  • Previous screening, evaluation, or intervention information
  • The learner's strengths, interests, and questions

5. Choose one next step

For a student, begin with the school's reading specialist, special-education contact, or evaluation process rather than assuming ability or disability from grades alone.
