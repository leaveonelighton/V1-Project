A Story Preservation Workbook

Why this exists

Important stories are often carried in memory until details begin to fade. This workbook helps preserve the people, places, choices, and ordinary acts of care that shaped a life - without turning private experience into public property.

Who it is for

Individuals, families, foster and adoptive families, elders, caregivers, community historians, and anyone entrusted with another person's memories.

Your meaningful next step

Choose one person or place. Record one memory today before trying to tell an entire life.

Begin with care

Story preservation is an act of listening. The goal is not to force a complete account, settle every disagreement, or create a polished public narrative. The goal is to make room for what a person chooses to remember and share.

Before you begin

  • Ask permission before recording, photographing, scanning, or sharing.
  • Let the storyteller skip any question or stop at any time.
  • Distinguish what was witnessed from what was heard later.
  • Do not pressure anyone to revisit trauma for the sake of completeness.
  • Decide where the material will be stored and who may access it.

A simple opening

You might say: "I would like to preserve some of the people and moments that mattered to you. We can begin wherever you want, and nothing has to be shared beyond the people you choose."

Digitizing checklist

  • Scan or photograph both sides.
  • Use a descriptive filename with an approximate date.
  • Keep an untouched master copy and a separate working copy.
  • Back up files in two different locations.
  • Record ownership and permission before publishing.

Permission and privacy

Possessing a photograph or story does not automatically mean it should be published. Consent can be limited by audience, format, duration, or subject.

Carry it forward

A preserved story becomes meaningful when it helps someone remember, understand, thank, repair, teach, or act. Choose a next step that respects the storyteller.

  • Give a copy to someone named in the story.
  • Label and safely store one photograph.
  • Record a follow-up conversation.
  • Write a thank-you note.
  • Preserve a recipe, saying, tradition, or practical skill.
  • Invite one person to leave a light on through an achievable act of care.
