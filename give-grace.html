What is grace?

Grace doesn't say what happened didn't matter. It doesn't erase consequences. It doesn't call wrong right.

Grace means the past doesn't have to be the only thing we're willing to see.

It leaves room for accountability. And then it leaves room for change.

When Does a Second Chance Become Real?

A person can serve a sentence, complete requirements, return home, and still discover that the consequences follow them into employment, housing, family life, and ordinary attempts to begin again.

This is personal for me. I lived part of that story.

I'm an employer

Responsible second-chance hiring does not mean ignoring risk. It means considering the record in context and seeing the person standing in front of you today.

I need a second chance

Know what your record says. Build evidence of what happened afterward: work, training, references, responsibility, and progress.

I want to help someone

Help with a résumé. Make an introduction. Give an honest reference. Offer a ride. Teach a skill. Listen long enough to understand.

Build the second file.

The first file may say what happened then. Build another file that shows what happened afterward.

  • Employment history and successful work
  • Education, training, licenses, and certifications
  • Promotions and accomplishments
  • Employment and character references
  • Rehabilitation programs completed
  • Community or volunteer service, when appropriate
  • Corrections to inaccurate criminal-history information

A second chance isn't asking someone to forget the evidence against you. It's asking them to consider the evidence for you, too.

Sometimes the first second chance is very small.

An interview. A returned phone call. A reference. A conversation. A second look.

We will never ask people to do everything. We will only invite them to do something.
